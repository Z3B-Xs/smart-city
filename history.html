History of Amman

from an ancient settlement to the capital of Jordan, more than 9,000 years of history

c. 7000 BC

Ain Ghazal

One of the earliest known settlements in the area, famous for its ancient plaster statues.

13TH CENTURY BC

Rabbath Ammon

The capital of the Ammonite kingdome. The name Amman comes from Ammon.

3RD CENTURY BC

Philadelphia

The city is renamed Philadelphia after Ptolemy II Philadelphus and grows as a Hellenistic center.

2ND CENTURY AD

A Roman city

Philadelphia thrives as one of the Decapolis cities. The Roman Threatre and the Temple of Hercules are built.

8TH CENTURY

The Umayyad era

An Umayyad palace complex rises in the Citadel hill, a sign of the city's regional importence.

1878 - 1902

A town reborn

Circassian settlers arrive in 1878, and the Hejaz Railway reaches Amman in 1902.

1921

A new capital

Emir Abdullah I makes Amman the capital of the Emirate of Transjordan.

1946

Independence

The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan is declared on 25 May, with Amman as its capital and Abdullah I as its first king.

1948 AND 1967

Waves of growth

Palestinian refugees arrive after 1948 and again in 1967, expanding the city far beyond its original hills.

1952

King Hussein

King Hussein takes the throne. Amman grows into a modern capital, and the University of Jordan opens in 1962.

1990 - 1991

Return from the Gulf

Hundreds of thousands of Jordanians and Palestinians return from the Gulf states, adding to the city's population.

1999

King Abdullah II

King Abdullah II accedes to the throne and leads a new phase of modernization.

2003 ONWARD

A refuge for the region

Amman receives Iraqi refugees, then Syrian refugees from 2011.

TODAY

A modern capital

Over four million people live in the greater area, where ancient sites like the Citadel and Roman Theatre sit beside a growing modern city.
